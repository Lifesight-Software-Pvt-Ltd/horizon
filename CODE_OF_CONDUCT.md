# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good
faith and is short on time, the same as you are.

## What we expect

- **Discuss the work, not the person.** Criticise a design, a number, or a
  diff. Do not characterise the author.
- **Say what you verified.** Disagreement backed by evidence is the most useful
  thing anyone contributes here. Disagreement backed by tone is not.
- **Accept that some proposals are turned down**, and that most rejections are
  about scope rather than quality. `CONTRIBUTING.md` says what is in scope so
  that this is predictable rather than personal.
- **Leave room for inexperience.** A question that seems obvious to you was not
  obvious to whoever asked it.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, or sustained hostility, in issues, pull requests, or any
  other project space.
- Insults or demeaning comments, including about someone's competence,
  background, or identity.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome personal attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly reopening a decision that
  has been made and explained.

This applies to everyone taking part — contributors, reviewers and maintainers
alike — in every project space, and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

If you see or experience something that breaches this, report it privately to
the repository owners through GitHub: open a report from the repository's
**Security** tab, or contact an organisation owner of
[Lifesight-Software-Pvt-Ltd](https://github.com/Lifesight-Software-Pvt-Ltd)
directly. Do not raise it in a public issue — that exposes the reporter and
rarely resolves anything.

Reports are handled confidentially. You will get an acknowledgement, and we
will tell you what we decided, though not necessarily the details of any action
taken about someone else.

## What happens next

Maintainers will look at what was said, in context, and respond
proportionately: usually a private word, sometimes an edited or removed
comment, and for serious or repeated breaches a temporary or permanent ban from
the project. Maintainers who breach this are held to it the same way.
